# Importing Assetto Corsa content into ApexSim — feasibility

*Research pass, 2026-09-26. Based on the `apexsim` repo at `1310b8b` and the AC install on pc-guido (`E:\SteamLibrary\steamapps\common\assettocorsa`: 194 cars, 74 tracks, all Kunos + mods, zero CSP-encrypted files).*

## Verdict

Feasible, and the formats are the easy part: every AC file involved is a simple, fully reverse-engineered binary or plain INI, and I parsed all 74 tracks and decrypted a `data.acd` on your machine with ~150 lines of Python. The hard part is that ApexSim's architecture makes three assumptions AC content violates:

1. **The server drives on a centerline, not a mesh.** The road is a ruled ribbon (centerline z + banking shear, per-node widths); the ground is a 2.5D heightfield used only off the asphalt. AC tracks are triangle-mesh physics. You can reduce an AC track to a centerline (the AI spline gives you widths for free), but you lose bumps, crown and camber, and the rendered AC road will not sit exactly on the simulated surface.
2. **The client cannot load content at runtime.** Every car and track is an editor-commandlet import into cooked Unreal assets plus a DataTable row. An AC importer today is a developer tool that ends in a repackage, not something a player runs. "Mountains of content without distributing it" needs runtime loading, which does not exist.
3. **Track visuals are generated, not imported.** The `.uescene.json` mesh path accepts arbitrary triangles but its materials are synthesized from a `family` key with the seven built-in ground texture sets; it cannot carry AC's diffuse/normal/mask textures. The only textured route into a level today is the GLB *prop* pipeline (Interchange), which works but is a detour.

Plus one non-technical one: Kunos' EULA and, separately, mod authors' terms. See the legal note at the end.

Rough shape of the work: a first drivable AC track and car through the existing editor pipeline is **2–3 weeks**; making it good (mesh physics, textured materials, texture liveries, real cockpits) is another **4–6 weeks**; making it a player-facing feature (runtime loading) is **4–8 weeks** of client work on top and a product decision about what the packaged game is.

## What AC content actually is (measured on your install)

**Track** (`content/tracks/ks_zandvoort`, 422 MB): `models.ini` lists the kn5 files. `ks_zandvoort.kn5` (277 MB) is the visual scene: 1,407 meshes, 1.5 M tris, 211 materials, 293 DDS textures (193 MB). `1.kn5` is more scenery, `2.kn5` (14 MB) is the **physics mesh**: 163 meshes, 157 k tris, names prefixed `NN<KEY>` where `KEY` matches `data/surfaces.ini` (`01TRM-ZNDV` road with FRICTION=0.98, `07KERB`, `02SAND`, `05WALL`, `01PITS` with IS_PITLANE=1, `01GRASS`, `06OUT`). Dummy nodes carry the race logic: `AC_START_n` (grid slots), `AC_PIT_n` (pit boxes), `AC_TIME_0/1/2_L/R` (start/finish and sector lines), `AC_HOTLAP_START_0`, `AC_AUDIO_*`, `AC_CREW_*`. `ai/fast_lane.ai` is the racing line: 2,695 points at 1.55 m, each with position, station, and an extra record with speed, radius, **sideLeft/sideRight distance to the road edge**, camber, normal, forward vector and grade. `ai/pit_lane.ai` is the pit spline (716 points). `data/` has `drs_zones.ini` (lap fractions), `sections.ini` (corner names by lap fraction), `cameras*.ini` (TV cams), `lighting.ini`, `groove.ini`, `map.ini`, `crew.ini`, `overlays.ini`. `ui/ui_track.json` has name, country, city, length, pit boxes, direction.

Across all 74 tracks: every kn5 parsed; 1–47 kn5 files each; 0.3–20 M tris; 20 MB–1.5 GB of DDS per track (Kunos circuits 200–600 MB); 67/74 have a `fast_lane.ai` (missing on `Istan`, `mountainroute_v09`, `topspeedarea` and four simtraxx stages); multi-layout tracks (`ks_nurburgring`, `bahrain`, `lemans_2017`…) have one `ai/` + `data/` per layout subfolder. Shaders used: `ksPerPixel`, `ksPerPixelNM`, `ksPerPixelMultiMap`, `ksPerPixelAT*`, `ksMultilayer_*` (terrain splat, 4 detail layers + mask), `ksTree`, `ksGrass`, `ksFlags`, `ksPerPixelReflection`, `ksWindscreen` — about 20 families, Blinn-Phong with `txDiffuse/txNormal/txMaps/txDetail/txMask` slots.

**Car** (`ks_porsche_911_gt3_r_2016`, 153 MB): `porsche_911_gt3_r.kn5` (44 MB: 263 meshes, 298 k tris, 74 DDS = 32 MB) plus `_lod_B/C/D.kn5` (57 k tris and down) with `lods.ini` switch distances. The node tree separates what ApexSim needs separated: `WHEEL_LF/RF/LR/RR` (with `RIM_`, `TYRE_`, `RIM_BLUR_` children), `SUSP_*`, `DISC_*`, `STEER_HR`, `COCKPIT_HR`/`COCKPIT_LR`, `DOOR_L/R`, `WING`, `FRONT_LIGHT`/`REAR_LIGHT`, `DAMAGE_GLASS_*`. Skin materials are named `EXT_SKIN_*` and take `Skin_00.dds` (4 MB, 2048²); `skins/<name>/` folders override textures by filename and carry `livery.png`, `preview.jpg`, `skin.ini`. `collider.kn5` is a 104-tri hull; `colliders.ini` has three boxes. `data.acd` (242 KB) decrypts to 70 files: `car.ini`, `engine.ini` + `power.lut`, `drivetrain.ini`, `suspensions.ini`, `tyres.ini` + wear/thermal LUTs, `aero.ini` + per-wing AoA/height LUTs, `brakes.ini`, `electronics.ini`, `setup.ini`, `damage.ini`, `ai.ini`, `lods.ini`, `lights.ini`, `cameras.ini`, `sounds.ini`, `fuel_cons.ini`, `colliders.ini`. Sound is an FMOD `.bank` (unusable, and ApexSim synthesizes anyway). All 194 cars use `data.acd`; none ship an unpacked `data/`.

**Formats.** kn5: magic `sc6969`, version int, texture table (name + embedded DDS blob), material table (name, shader, float props, texture slots), then a node tree of dummies (4×4 matrix), meshes (44-byte vertices: pos, normal, uv, tangent; u16 indices; material id; LOD in/out; bounding sphere) and skinned meshes. data.acd: `[u32 name_len][name][u32 len][len*4 bytes]`, one byte in four is payload, XOR-free additive cipher whose key is eight small arithmetic hashes of the car folder name — published as a GPL Python library and reimplemented in 40 lines. fast_lane.ai: 16-byte header, `count × (x,y,z,length,id)`, then `count × 18 floats`. Coordinate frame: Y-up and mirrored relative to glTF's right-handed frame (converters negate one axis — verify on one track); ApexSim wants Z-up, origin on the start line, +Y left, ~5 m nodes.

## What ApexSim has today (the relevant facts)

Server: `track_loader.rs` reads the YAML (`name`, `nodes[x,y,z,width_left,width_right,banking,friction,surface_type]`, `raceline`, `drs_zones` as stations in metres, `spawn_points` indexed into the ~1 m interpolated centerline with world-frame offsets, `sectors`, `checkpoints`, `closed_loop`, `metadata`) and three optional msgpack sidecars: `.ground` (i16 cm heights on a 4 m grid, ±327 m range — the Nordschleife already sits at −289 m), `.curbs` (per-metre left/right curb and run-off widths in cm), `.walls` (2D segments with base z, height and kind Armco/Tires/Concrete/Kerb, SAT box collision). `surface_type` is stored but has no physics effect; grip is `friction` × the Road/Curb/Runoff/Off constants. On-road height is *always* centerline z − lateral × sin(banking) (`physics.rs surface_elevation`); the heightfield only applies past the road edge. There is an unfinished `APEXSIM_SURFACE_QUERY_BACKEND=mesh` seam (`physics.rs:138–166, 2038–2066`) that currently falls back to the centerline — that is where mesh physics would plug in. Pit lane exists as a data type but is never simulated.

Car: `car_loader.rs` needs `id, name, version, model` and `[physics]{mass_kg, max_engine_force_n, max_brake_force_n, drag_coefficient, grip_coefficient, max_steering_angle_rad, wheelbase_m}`; everything else defaults. The tyre takes exactly one number from the file (`grip_coefficient`; peak force = grip × load, no load sensitivity, fixed magic-formula shapes). Suspension is a per-axle zero-sum load term with no body roll/pitch; aero is `Cd·A` plus front/rear lift coefficients on the same area; drivetrain FWD/RWD/AWD(40/60 fixed); no differential model (parsed, ignored); torque curve from `[[engine.torque_curve]]`; hybrid is simulated. No camber, toe, geometry, ride height, tyre thermal/wear effect, brake fade, turbo model, aero maps.

Client: `ApexCarImport` takes a GLB (metres, nose −Y, driver +X, **no wheels**, all meshes combined, no LODs, glTF PBR materials kept as authored, named slots `car_paint/car_accent/car_logo/car_glass/car_headlight/car_taillight/car_brakelight/car_rainlight` driven by the game) and writes a `DT_CarCatalog` row keyed by UUID. Wheels are one shared GLB per `[wheels].model`, drawn four times and scaled per axle. Liveries are colour/metallic on `car_paint`/`car_accent` plus a texture swap on `car_logo` only. The cockpit is synthesized (wheel, dash, mirrors) from mesh bounds, with per-car overrides in the catalog row. `ApexTrackImport` reads only `*.uescene.json` (one material key + one UV set per mesh, materials generated per family from the built-in ground sets, decals the only textured exception) and builds one level per track; `ApexPropImport` imports any GLB with its PBR textures via Interchange and `.ats` props place them. Interchange is editor-only (`ApexTrackEditor.Build.cs`); the runtime module has no glTF dependency, no `Plugins/` dir, `bCookAll=True`, no pak/DLC/GameFeature setup. Both sides CRC-32 the source file and toast on mismatch.

## Tracks: AC → ApexSim mapping

| AC source | ApexSim target | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `fast_lane.ai` (pos + sideLeft/sideRight) | YAML `nodes` (centerline = AI point shifted by (sideR−sideL)/2 along the lateral; `width_left/right`; z; resampled to 5 m), `raceline` (the AI line itself) | **Now** — pure data work. Watch garbage side values (Zandvoort has sideLeft = 4.18e6 on some points; clamp/interpolate). 7/74 tracks have no AI file and would need a centerline from the road mesh (medial axis of `*ROAD*` tris) — harder, defer. |
| Road physics mesh cross-slope | `banking` per node | **Easy** — raycast the road mesh at both edges per station. |
| `surfaces.ini` FRICTION per KEY, mesh prefixes | `friction` / `surface_type` per node | **Now** for the road; per-node only, so a kerb's 0.96 vs road 0.98 is lost (ApexSim uses its own curb constant). |
| `AC_TIME_0/1/2_L/R` dummies | `sectors` (2 node indices), start/finish = origin | **Now**. (Note: `ats-smooth`/`ats-bank` drop `sectors` when they rewrite the YAML — don't run them on imported tracks, or fix `track-editor/core/src/track_data.rs`.) |
| `AC_START_n` | `spawn_points` (nearest interpolated index + world offset) | **Now**. Gives the real grid instead of the generated 16-slot one. |
| `AC_PIT_n`, `pit_lane.ai`, `IS_PITLANE` surfaces | pit lane | **No home** — server never simulates a pit lane; `.ats` pit lane is visual only. Skip. |
| `drs_zones.ini` (lap fractions) | `drs_zones` (× length_m) | **Now**. |
| `ui_track.json` | `metadata` (name, country, city, length) | **Now**. |
| `*WALL*` physics meshes | `.walls` msgpack (2D segments, z, height, kind) | **Easy** — project wall tris to segments per 4 m, kind from name/surface. `bake_walls` today only knows kit footprints; this replaces it for imported tracks. |
| `*GRASS/SAND/OUT*` meshes | `.ground` msgpack (4 m grid, i16 cm) | **Easy** — rasterize; one-valued field so overpasses become whichever level wins (Suzuka crossover works today only because the editor handles the deck as a special case). |
| `*KERB*` meshes | `.curbs` msgpack (per-metre widths) | **Easy** — measure kerb extent beside the road edge per station. |
| Road mesh bumps, crown, camber, kerb profiles | server road surface | **Blocker (architectural)** — the ribbon can't carry them. Fix = implement the mesh backend behind the existing seam: uniform grid over road/kerb/runoff tris, per-wheel ray/height query, friction from `surfaces.ini`; keep the centerline for stations, AI, track limits. 1–2 weeks, and the rendered road then matches physics exactly. Until then the AC road mesh must be **hidden** and replaced by ApexSim's generated ribbon (sits on the sim surface, looks like a different track at the edges), or shown with visible height mismatches. |
| Visual kn5 meshes + DDS textures | level geometry | **Detour exists**: split the scene by material into GLBs (DDS→PNG, PBR approximation), import via `ApexPropImport`, place with identity-transform `misc` props in the `.ats`, run the level build with `-SkipDress`. Proper route = new `uescene` mesh family "textured" that carries albedo/normal/mask paths and a matching material (few days in `ApexTrackAssetBuilder.cpp`). |
| `ks*` shaders (`ksMultilayer` terrain splats, `ksTree`, `ksGrass`, `ksFlags`, `ksWindscreen`…) | UE materials | **Hard-ish** — glTF PBR can express `ksPerPixel*` (diffuse+normal, spec-exp→roughness) but not multilayer splats or the `txMaps` channel packing. Either bake, or write ~6 UE material functions once and have the importer emit parameters. 1–2 weeks for "looks like AC". |
| 1.5–8 M tris, 200–600 MB DDS per track | cooked level | **Works but heavy**: current levels have Nanite off and are cut into 250 m sections; enable Nanite for opaque scenery, keep AC LOD in/out distances (kn5 carries them). Package grows by the track's size. |
| `cameras.ini`, `sections.ini`, `lighting.ini`, `groove`, `crew`, `overlays` | — | Ignore; ApexSim's TV director, corner naming and lighting are its own. |

## The real blockers, ranked

**1. No runtime content loading in the client.** *(Investigated separately in `docs/RUNTIME_CONTENT_LOADING.md`: tracks are already "data + kit", so a runtime loader is ~3–4 weeks and removes the editor from the track loop entirely; cars ~2–3 weeks more.)* This is what stands between "an importer" and "mountains of content". Today the packaged game has cooked levels, cooked car meshes and DataTable catalogs; a new track means the editor commandlets and `build_release.ps1`. For *you* that's fine (an importer that emits `content/cars/<x>/car.toml` + GLB and `content/tracks/real/<X>.yaml` + sidecars + scenery GLBs, then the normal pipeline). For *players*, without redistributing Kunos assets, every player must run the importer on their own AC install and the client must load the result at runtime: glTFRuntime (MIT plugin, UE5) for meshes and textures, a procedural/dynamic-mesh level built from a uescene-like JSON instead of a cooked `.umap`, catalogs read from files instead of `DT_*` rows, and a content directory the packaged game scans like the server does. Realistically 4–8 weeks and it changes what the client *is*. The server side is already there: it scans `content/` recursively at startup and drives from the files.

**2. Centerline physics vs mesh physics.** Covered above. The importer can produce a drivable approximation now (that is exactly what the 26 existing circuits are), but you throw away the thing that makes AC tracks feel like AC tracks — the road surface — and the rendered road can't be the AC mesh unless the sim drives on it. The `MeshHeightfield` seam is the right place; the tri counts (150 k–1.5 M physics tris) are trivial for a grid-indexed height query at 240 Hz × 4 wheels.

**3. Track materials.** `uescene` can't carry textures, `.ats` props can. Either add a textured mesh family (small) or accept the prop detour (works today, ugly in the pipeline). Add ~6 UE material functions for the `ks*` families if you want it to look like AC and not like a glTF viewer.

**4. Liveries are colours, not textures.** Small client change, but until it lands AC skins don't exist.

**5. Physics fidelity ceiling.** AC's tyre (load-sensitive μ, thermal, pressure, flex, camber), suspension geometry, aero maps and differential have no counterparts. Imported cars need re-balancing per class with the grip probe, and a Kunos-tuned setup won't mean anything. Not a blocker for "playable", a blocker for "the AC car".

**6. Sidecar limits.** `.ground` is i16 cm (±327 m; fine for anything but long point-to-point stages like `simtraxx_transfagarasan` — 2 km of climb — which would need the datum moved or an f32 field) and single-valued (overpasses). `.walls` is 2D with one height band per segment; AC wall meshes are arbitrary geometry, so bridges/tunnels need care.

**7. Legal.** Below.

## Recommended path

Phase 0 (a few days): a Python `ac2apex` tool in `scripts/`: kn5 reader, acd decrypt, fast_lane parser, GLB writer (via `pygltflib` or trimesh), DDS→PNG. Everything it needs is in the appendix.

Phase 1 — track, developer-only (1–2 weeks): emit YAML from `fast_lane.ai` + timing dummies + `ui_track.json`; bake `.walls/.ground/.curbs` directly from the physics kn5 (skip `ats-export`'s sidecars, or add an `--external-sidecars` flag so it doesn't overwrite them); emit scenery GLBs with the road/kerb/grass physics meshes and the visual road *hidden*, placed as props in a seeded `.ats`; run the normal level build with `-SkipDress`. Result: a drivable Zandvoort with AC's buildings, trees and stands around ApexSim's road ribbon. Then run the AI survey.

Phase 2 — car (1 week): acd→`car.toml` with the turbo and aero collapses above; kn5→body GLB + wheel GLB with slot renames; one skin as livery 0. Balance with the grip probe.

Phase 3 — make it right (4–6 weeks): mesh-backed surface query on the server and show the real AC road; textured mesh family + `ks*` materials; skin-texture liveries; own-cockpit flag with DRIVEREYES and STEER_HR; Nanite on imported scenery.

Phase 4 — decide on runtime loading. This is the product question, not an importer question.

## Alternative for tracks: the kn5 as a survey, not as content

*Added after discussion: derive a dossier + centerline + terrain from the kn5, then run the normal ApexSim pipeline so the result is a regular ApexSim track.*

This is the better plan for tracks, and it needs **no engine changes**. The existing pipeline already consumes exactly three inputs before `ats-dress`/`ats-export` take over: the YAML centerline, `<Stem>.layout.json` (the dossier — `track-editor/core/src/layout.rs`: corners, pit road, stands, structures, crossings, landmarks, woods, barrier lines, areas) and `<Stem>.dem.msgpack` (two float height grids). Today those come from a GPS trace, OpenStreetMap and Copernicus GLO-30; every one of them can be read off the kn5 *more accurately*:

| Pipeline input | From OSM/DEM today | From the kn5 |
|---|---|---|
| Centerline, widths | OSM way + `width` tags, ~5 m | `fast_lane.ai` + road mesh edges, cm-accurate per metre; 7/74 tracks need a medial axis of the road polygon instead |
| Elevation | 30 m DEM, reads tree canopy on wooded circuits | raycast the road mesh at each node: the real profile |
| Banking | `ats-bank` estimate | measured cross-slope of the road mesh per node |
| Sectors, grid, start line, DRS, corner names | hand tables | `AC_TIME_*`, `AC_START_*`, `drs_zones.ini`, `sections.ini` |
| Kerbs, gravel traps, run-off asphalt, sand (`.ats` surfaces/curbs) | `seed_scene.py` (grass + curbs only; the shipped circuits' run-off was a one-off) | lateral extent of each named physics surface per station → exact `Surface`/`Curb` spans |
| Barrier lines | OSM `barrier=*` where mapped | `*WALL*` meshes → `Line` runs with kind from the surface/name |
| Stands, buildings, pit building | OSM footprints + `levels`, `MANUAL_STANDS` | visual-mesh bounding boxes per named node (footprint, height, yaw, side, station); stands vs buildings by name heuristics (`tribuna`, `stand`, `grandstand`, `box`, `pit`) |
| Woods / tree belts | OSM `landuse=forest` | positions of `ksTree`-shaded meshes → alpha-shape polygons |
| Pit lane | `MANUAL_PIT_LANE` | `pit_lane.ai` + `PITS` surface |
| Crossings, landmarks | `MANUAL_*` | bridges from meshes spanning the road; landmarks only where the kit has a kind |
| Terrain (`.dem.msgpack`) | GLO-30 | rasterize GRASS/SAND/OUT meshes at 1–2 m; datum is the start line so the ±327 m `.ground` limit only bites on long hill climbs |

What comes out is a track dressed from the ApexSim kit at the real circuit's geometry: correct road, correct kerbs and traps, barriers in the right place, buildings and stands of the right size on the right side, trees where the trees are. Everything visual is kit — that is the same art direction as the 26 shipped circuits, and it also means **nothing from Kunos is rendered or shipped**: the output is derived measurements, the same category as the OSM dossier. This makes the legal position far cleaner than mesh import (still not nothing for Kunos' *fictional* circuits — Highlands, Black Cat County, Drift — where the layout itself is their design).

What is lost, honestly: all AC visual identity (textures, specific architecture, signage, trackside detail) — you get "ApexSim's Zandvoort", not "AC's Zandvoort"; road micro-surface (bumps, crown, camber variations) and 3D kerb profiles, exactly as on the existing tracks, because the server still drives on the ribbon; landmarks with no kit equivalent; AC's TV cameras (ApexSim has its own director). Nothing that the *simulation* uses is lost — the sim never used more than the centerline anyway.

What is gained over the current OSM route: real elevation and banking instead of DEM guesses, per-metre widths, exact run-off layouts, and access to circuits OSM cannot give you — historic layouts (`ks_monza66`, `ks_silverstone1967`, `donington1938`, `bridgehampton`, `deutschlandring`, `targa-florio`) and fictional ones.

Effort: pure Python in `scripts/` (an `ac_survey.py` next to `osm_layout.py`/`dem_fetch.py`), ~1–2 weeks to a first good circuit, then the standard `ats-smooth` → `ats-bank` → `seed_scene` → `build_track_levels.ps1` run — with two fixes: teach `ats-smooth`/`ats-bank` not to drop `sectors` (`track-editor/core/src/track_data.rs`), and let `seed_scene.py` accept authored surfaces/curbs instead of overwriting them. Runtime loading is unchanged (still an editor pipeline), but this is a content-authoring accelerator rather than a mod loader, so that matters less: the tracks it produces are ordinary ApexSim tracks that can be shipped like the others.

## Legal note (not legal advice)

The Kunos EULA on Steam grants a personal, single-PC licence, says "LICENSEE may not use, disclose, modify, reproduce or distribute the SOFTWARE except as expressly permitted", forbids public/commercial use without written permission, and says nothing explicit about reverse engineering or converting assets. Converting Kunos content for your own use on your own machine is the same category as the existing AC mod tools (Content Manager unpacks kn5/acd routinely); *distributing* converted Kunos content is clearly out, and shipping a converter as a feature of a product with a commercial licence path is the grey zone — Kunos has historically tolerated modding *for* AC and objected to ripping *from* it. Mod content (simtraxx, acu, dcgp, the 2023 layouts on your disk) carries the authors' own terms, and most modding communities treat cross-game conversion without permission as ripping. The safe framing is the one you described: a tool the user runs locally on content they own, output never leaves their machine, nothing bundled with ApexSim. Note also that the encryption is trivial by design and CSP-encrypted mod kn5s (none on your install, common in paid mods) should be refused outright rather than broken.

## Appendix: format facts and tools

**kn5 layout.** `sc6969`, i32 version (6 on everything here; if >5 one extra i32), i32 texture count then per texture `i32 active, str name, i32 size, bytes` (DDS: BC1/BC3/BC5 in the wild, 2048² skins), i32 material count then per material `str name, str shader, u8 alphaBlend, u8 alphaTested, i32 depthMode, i32 nprops × (str, f32, 36 bytes), i32 nslots × (str slot, i32, str texture)`, then the node tree: `i32 type, str name, i32 children, u8 active`; type 1 = 16 f32 matrix; type 2 = `u8×3, i32 nverts × 44 bytes (pos3, nrm3, uv2, tan3), i32 nidx × u16, i32 material, i32 layer, f32 lodIn, f32 lodOut, f32×4 sphere, u8 renderable`; type 3 = skinned (`i32 nbones × (str, 64 bytes)`, 76-byte verts). Strings are `i32 len + bytes`. The parser is in `~/work/kn5.py` on pc-guido's session VM (throwaway; rewrite into the tool).

**data.acd.** Optional 8-byte header (`A9 FB FF FF` + 4), then records `i32 name_len, name, i32 n, n×4 bytes`; payload byte = `(b[4k] − key[k mod len]) mod 256`; key = eight hashes of the lower-cased folder name joined with `-` (see philippkosarev/acd, GPL-2 — reimplement, don't vendor). LUTs are `x|y` lines; INIs are plain with `;` comments.

**fast_lane.ai.** Header `i32 version(7), i32 count, i32 lapTime, i32 sampleCount`; `count × (f32 x, y, z, length, i32 id)`; `count × 18 f32`: speed, gas, brake, obsoleteLatG, radius, sideLeft, sideRight, camber, direction, normal xyz, length, forward xyz, tag, grade (the record read on Zandvoort appeared shifted by one float — confirm the offset against a second track before trusting sideLeft/sideRight).

**surfaces.ini** keys per surface: `KEY, FRICTION, DAMPING, WAV, FF_EFFECT, DIRT_ADDITIVE, IS_VALID_TRACK, SIN_HEIGHT/LENGTH (fake bumps), IS_PITLANE, VIBRATION_GAIN/LENGTH, BLACK_FLAG_TIME`. A physics mesh named `NNKEYxx` uses the surface whose KEY is a prefix after the two digits.

**Existing open tools worth reading, not shipping:** `semiloker/assetto-corsa-gltf` (MIT, Python; kn5→glTF with node hierarchy, DDS→PNG, PBR approximations incl. clearcoat for paint; refuses encrypted kn5; no decimation — "Magione is a 165 MB buffer"), `MarvinSt/kn5-obj-converter`, `moppius/blender-assetto-corsa-tools`, `leBluem/io_import_accsv` (AI/CSV into Blender), `philippkosarev/acd` (GPL-2), `rdeioris/glTFRuntime` (UE runtime glTF, MIT). A Godot write-up of the same exercise reports the usual pain: material loss on FBX, z-fighting from groove meshes, `ksGrass`/tree shaders looking like aerial photos without custom materials, and hand-pruning trees and crowds for performance.

**ApexSim files an importer touches:** `server/src/track_loader.rs`, `ground.rs`, `curbs.rs`, `walls.rs`, `car_loader.rs`, `physics.rs` (surface seam), `track-editor/core/src/ue_export_io.rs` (sidecar writer), `ue_export.rs` (`bake_walls`, mesh families), `game-unreal/Source/ApexTrackEditor/Private/ApexTrackAssetBuilder.cpp` (materials), `ApexCarImportCommandlet.cpp`, `game-unreal/Source/ApexSim/.../ApexCarLivery.cpp`, `ApexCockpitLayout.*`.
